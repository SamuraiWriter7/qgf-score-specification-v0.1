## 1. Purpose

This document defines a minimal measurement procedure for calculating and interpreting a QGF Score.

QGF Score estimates the gravitational field created by a question.

A question may attract:

- AI responses
- structural derivatives
- trace records
- structure fingerprints
- lineage relations
- articles
- protocols
- repositories
- further questions

The purpose of this procedure is to prevent arbitrary scoring.

QGF should not be treated as a subjective impression, popularity ranking, or authority claim.

It should be treated as a structured observation process.

---

## 2. Measurement Principle

QGF measurement follows five principles.

### 2.1 Evidence First

A QGF Score should be based on observable evidence.

Examples:

- timestamped question origin
- article records
- GitHub repository records
- trace records
- structure fingerprints
- lineage references
- derivative specifications
- GPT or agent implementations
- cross-AI responses

A score without evidence should be marked as provisional.

---

### 2.2 Estimate, Not Verdict

QGF Score is an estimate.

It does not prove:

- legal authorship
- absolute originality
- copyright ownership
- entitlement to payment
- intellectual superiority
- platform authority

QGF measures field strength, not ownership.

---

### 2.3 Multi-Reviewer Preference

A QGF Score should not depend on a single evaluator when possible.

Recommended evaluators:

- human reviewer
- multiple AI models
- domain expert
- repository maintainer
- trace reviewer

For higher-confidence measurement, use at least three independent reviewers or AI systems.

---

### 2.4 Median over Single Output

When multiple reviewers provide parameter estimates, the median value is preferred.

This reduces distortion caused by:

- overestimation
- underestimation
- model bias
- emotional interpretation
- platform-specific tendency

Recommended aggregation:

```text
final_parameter_value = median(reviewer_values)

2.5 Tolerance Band over Exact Point

QGF measurement should allow uncertainty.

A score should preferably be expressed with a tolerance band.

Example:

QGF Score: 3.42
Tolerance Band: ±0.25
Field Class: Resonant Field

This means the score is not a single absolute point.

It is a measured field range.

4. Step-by-Step Procedure

Record the question being measured.

Minimum fields:

language: "en"
status: "draft"

The question should be clear enough to evaluate.

If the question is vague, fragmented, or reconstructed after the fact, mark the score as provisional.

Step 2: Collect Evidence

Collect evidence connected to the question.

Recommended evidence categories:

1. Origin Evidence
2. Trace Evidence
3. Structural Evidence
4. Lineage Evidence
5. Resonance Evidence
6. Derivative Output Evidence
7. Noise Evidence

Examples:

Evidence that later outputs relate to the question.

Examples:

2.5 Resonance Evidence

Evidence that the question resonates across systems.

Examples:

multiple AI models responding similarly

Evidence that the question generated concrete outputs.

Examples:

Examples:

Step 3: Score Core Parameters

Score each core parameter.

Recommended range:

5. Parameter Scoring Guide
5.1 S — Source Strength

Source Strength measures how clearly the origin point of the question is recorded.

S = 0.0 – 1.0

Suggested scoring:

0.0 – 0.2 : origin unclear or reconstructed
0.3 – 0.4 : weak origin reference
0.5 – 0.6 : identifiable origin, limited evidence
0.7 – 0.8 : clear timestamped origin
0.9 – 1.0 : strong origin with trace / repository / public record

High S requires strong anchoring.

5.2 D — Depth

Depth measures how deeply the question reaches into underlying structures.

D = 0.0 – 1.0

Suggested scoring:

0.0 – 0.2 : surface-level question
0.3 – 0.4 : useful but limited question
0.5 – 0.6 : conceptually meaningful question
0.7 – 0.8 : structural or cross-domain question
0.9 – 1.0 : foundational question capable of generating theory

High D means the question opens a field rather than closing with a simple answer.

5.3 I — Influence

Influence measures how much the question generates further structures.

I = 0.0 – 1.0

Suggested scoring:

0.0 – 0.2 : little or no derivative output
0.3 – 0.4 : small discussion or local reuse
0.5 – 0.6 : several related outputs
0.7 – 0.8 : multiple structured derivatives
0.9 – 1.0 : strong derivative ecosystem across media or systems

Influence should be based on generated structure, not simple visibility.

5.4 C — Coherence

Coherence measures how internally consistent and reusable the question structure is.

C = 0.0 – 1.0

Suggested scoring:

0.0 – 0.2 : incoherent or contradictory
0.3 – 0.4 : partially coherent
0.5 – 0.6 : understandable but not reusable
0.7 – 0.8 : structurally consistent and reusable
0.9 – 1.0 : highly coherent, protocol-ready, schema-ready, or framework-ready

High C means the question can be converted into reusable structure.

5.5 T — Temporal Distance

Temporal Distance measures the time distance from the question’s origin.

T >= 1.0

Suggested scoring:

1.0 : current or actively developing
1.2 : recent and continuing
1.5 : short-term persistence
2.0 : medium-term persistence
3.0 : long-term historical persistence

T does not mean importance.

It represents time exposure.

Time decay is handled through β.

5.6 N — Noise Level

Noise Level measures distortion around the question.

N >= 1.0

Suggested scoring:

1.0 – 1.2 : very low noise
1.3 – 1.5 : manageable noise
1.6 – 2.0 : moderate noise
2.1 – 3.0 : high noise
3.0+      : severe distortion

Noise weakens the field.

However, strong structure may reduce noise sensitivity through γ.

5.7 α — Resonance Exponent

The Resonance Exponent measures nonlinear resonance.

α = 1.0 – 2.0

Suggested scoring:

1.0 – 1.1 : little resonance
1.2 – 1.3 : local resonance
1.4 – 1.5 : cross-domain resonance
1.6 – 1.7 : multi-system resonance
1.8 – 2.0 : strong civilizational or multi-AI resonance

High α means the question does not merely spread.

It amplifies.

5.8 β — Temporal Decay

Temporal Decay controls how strongly time weakens the field.

β = 0.5 – 2.0

Suggested scoring:

0.5 – 0.7 : strong persistence; trace and lineage reduce decay
0.8 – 1.0 : moderate persistence
1.1 – 1.4 : normal decay
1.5 – 2.0 : rapid decay

Lower β means the question resists time decay.

A traced question lives longer.

5.9 γ — Noise Sensitivity

Noise Sensitivity controls how strongly noise weakens the field.

γ = 0.5 – 2.0

Suggested scoring:

0.5 – 0.7 : strong noise resistance
0.8 – 1.0 : moderate noise resistance
1.1 – 1.4 : normal noise sensitivity
1.5 – 2.0 : high vulnerability to noise

Lower γ means the question retains structure despite distortion.

7. Aggregation Procedure

When multiple reviewer scores exist, aggregate each parameter separately.

Example:

Reviewer A: S = 0.90
Reviewer B: S = 0.86
Reviewer C: S = 0.94

Median S = 0.90

Recommended method:

final_S     = median(S values)
final_D     = median(D values)
final_I     = median(I values)
final_C     = median(C values)
final_T     = median(T values)
final_N     = median(N values)
final_alpha = median(alpha values)
final_beta  = median(beta values)
final_gamma = median(gamma values)

Do not average everything blindly.

Median is preferred because QGF is sensitive to extreme estimates.

8. Formula Calculation

After aggregation, calculate raw gravity.

G_raw = ((S × D × I × C)^α) / (T^β × N^γ)

If a calibration factor is used:

G_calibrated = G_raw × K

Then calculate QGF Score:

QGF = log(1 + G_calibrated)

If no calibration factor is used:

QGF = log(1 + G_raw)

The calibration factor must be documented.

9. Calibration Rule

Calibration may be used because normalized values often produce small raw gravity values.

Recommended default:

K = 40.0

The purpose of calibration is not to inflate scores.

It is used to map normalized parameters into human-readable field classes.

Any QGF object using calibration must include:

calibration:
  calibration_factor_K: 40.0
  reason: "Normalized parameters between 0.0 and 1.0 produce small raw values. Calibration makes field classes interpretable without changing relative structure."
10. Tolerance Band

A QGF Score should include an uncertainty band when possible.

Recommended methods:

10.1 Simple Reviewer Range

Use the spread between reviewer scores.

tolerance_band = max(reviewer_qgf_scores) - min(reviewer_qgf_scores)

Then express as:

QGF Score: 3.42
Tolerance Band: ±0.25
10.2 Parameter Confidence Method

If reviewer confidence is available, lower confidence produces a wider band.

Example:

High confidence   : ±0.10 – ±0.20
Medium confidence : ±0.20 – ±0.40
Low confidence    : ±0.40+
10.3 Field Class Boundary Caution

If a score is close to a field boundary, mark it as transitional.

Example:

QGF Score: 2.95
Field Class: Structural Field / Resonant Field boundary

This avoids false precision.

11. Field Class Assignment

Use the following classes.

0.0 – 0.9 : Weak Field
1.0 – 1.9 : Local Field
2.0 – 2.9 : Structural Field
3.0 – 3.9 : Resonant Field
4.0 – 4.9 : Civilizational Field
5.0+      : Gravity Well

Field class should be interpreted carefully.

A higher class does not mean a “better” question.

It means a stronger measured gravitational field.

For a score to be considered more than provisional, it should include at least:

For Resonant Field or higher, recommended evidence includes:

1 origin evidence

For Gravity Well classification, recommended evidence includes:

clear origin
multiple derivative structures

low or controlled noise
traceable continuation over time
human or multi-agent review
13. Review Status

A QGF measurement may have one of the following statuses.

draft        : early estimate
sample       : illustrative example
experimental : real measurement, not yet reviewed
reviewed     : reviewed by human or multi-agent process
deprecated   : no longer recommended
superseded   : replaced by a newer measurement

15. Risk Controls

QGF measurement can be misused if treated as authority.

Required cautions:

Do not use QGF as legal proof.
Do not use QGF as automatic payment allocation.
Do not use QGF to rank creators absolutely.
Do not use QGF to erase context.
Do not use QGF to claim ownership over broad questions.
Do not use QGF without evidence.
Do not hide calibration assumptions.

QGF is a measurement aid.

It is not a throne.

17. Summary

QGF measurement should proceed from evidence to parameters, from parameters to formula, from formula to field class, and from field class to cautious interpretation.

The central rule is:

Measure the field, not the ego.

A strong question does not need to claim authority.

It leaves traces, generates structure, attracts lineage, resists noise, and continues to produce new questions.

QGF Measurement Procedure v0.1 exists to observe that process with structure, restraint, and reproducibility.
